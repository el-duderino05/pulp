# Chapter Three

## The Keeper’s Daughter

The lamp at [Gull Point]{.smallcaps} had burned every night for *forty* years. Maren knew its rhythm better than her own heartbeat: **two long, one short**.

Her father had crossed out the old schedule — ~~dusk plus ten~~ — and written a new one in the margin, [underlined twice]{.underline}, as if the sea might argue.

### The logbook

Each entry followed the same form:

1. the time, to the minute;
2. the wind, in knots;
3. anything ***unusual***.

On the inside cover he had copied a line from his own father:

> Keep the glass clean and the oil full.\
> The rest is weather.
>
> — J. H., 1931

#### Supplies

The cellar held what they needed for winter:

- paraffin, forty gallons
- salt fish and hard bread
- spare wicks
  - cotton
  - asbestos, which nobody trusted

&nbsp;

##### A note on the chemistry

Paraffin is mostly C~12~H~26~, and the flame reached nearly 10^3^ degrees at its core.
